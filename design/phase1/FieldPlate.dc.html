A natural history of the gut, assembled from everything already published.

Seventeen thousand gut microbiomes, eight hundred host species, and a field-wide audit of whether any of it can be reused. Three projects, one library.

I. GMToL
Plate I. Host phylogeny, time-calibrated, with phylum composition at the tips.
Gut Microbiome Tree of Life
Gut microbiomes compiled across the animal kingdom, from chordates to arthropods to annelids, curated for even coverage of phylogeny, diet, ecology and geography.

Plate II. Global coverage of the human collection to date.
Human Microbiome Tree of Life
The human-focused successor: a global gut microbiome atlas that deliberately reaches the populations the literature has left out.

III. MMC
Plate III. Of 60 studies, those with accessions, and those truly reusable.
Microbiome Metadata Crisis
A systematic review of the field's own record-keeping. Most published microbiome data is public but not reusable; we measured how much, and what it would take to fix.
